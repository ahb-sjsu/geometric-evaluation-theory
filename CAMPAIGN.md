## G2. Hull law on a public dataset with a known consequence map

The hull law (Theorem 4(b)) is the cheapest test of the theory. It needs a world whose consequence map is known and menus of at least three alternatives whose consequences are not collinear.
Candidate world. Spatial voting data with respondent-placed candidate positions on two or more issue scales and respondent rankings or thermometer ratings of four or more candidates (American National Election Studies waves with multi-candidate placements). The consequence map is the respondent's own placement of each candidate; the evaluator is the respondent.
Registration. Seal the wave, the issue scales, the candidate set, the ranking source, the tie rule, the exclusion rules, and the null before touching the file.
Statistic. The fraction of respondents whose ranking violates the hull law at least once, against the same fraction under the null that rankings are shuffled within respondent.
Bar. Violation rate below the null by a preregistered margin, with a preregistered minimum number of respondents whose candidate placements are in general position (anti-vacuity: collinear placements make the law empty, and the probe must show how many respondents have a genuinely two-dimensional menu).
What falsifies. Violation rate at or above the null. That would falsify every convex metric of evaluation for that population, not only the quadratic one.
Record, 2026-09-07. Data: ANES 1972 Time Series (ICPSR 7010) Stata release, downloaded by the owner, gitignored. The codebook shows only Nixon, McGovern, Wallace and the two parties placed on issue scales in 1972, so the menu is those five objects on the post-election Form II scales; the primary pair is liberal-conservative with guaranteed jobs, and the five other pairs of the four Form II scales are registered replications. ## G3. Threshold tracks budget, computational evaluator

Prediction (i) of the paper. The indifference threshold of the induced semiorder is the resolution budget.
World. A computational evaluator whose consequence map is known and whose resolution can be set, for example a language-model judge comparing two options whose consequences are rendered at a controlled numeric precision or a fixed context budget, or a quantized scorer with a settable bit width.
Registration. Seal the evaluator, the consequence space, the resolution ladder, the pair set, the estimator of the just-noticeable difference, and the bars.
Bar. The estimated threshold is monotone in the resolution ladder and the ordering of well-separated pairs (separated by more than the largest threshold) is unchanged across the ladder.
What falsifies. A threshold that does not move with resolution, or a reordering of well-separated pairs under a resolution change.
Registration, 2026-09-08 to 2026-09-09. `experiments/G3/`. The evaluator is a language-model scorer, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on Atlas, asked how far a value is from a target and answering with a number; the induced order compares reported distances and equal reports are indifference. Three budgets set independently: the decimals shown (0 to 3), the report length in generated tokens (this tokenizer emits one digit per token, so with two-digit distances k tokens afford a resolution of 10, 1, 1, 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 for k = 1 to 6), and the weight precision (bfloat16, 8-bit, 4-bit through bitsandbytes 0.50.2, which loads on the Volta GPU). The threshold of a cell is the gap between two options' distances at which the scorer orders 90 percent of pairs correctly, interpolated in log gap. Two instruments tried before it are kept in the record: a pairwise chooser read from answer-letter logits was vacuous with options on both sides of the target (the model picks the larger number) and had a floor of 7.5 with options on one side (first-position bias), and the instrument was reading the wrong letter tokens under the chat template; a pairwise chooser measures its position bias before it measures a threshold. The self-test recovers the threshold of a rounding scorer at three grid steps; the probe finds the scorer exact to three decimals with a floor of 0.00087; the pilot's eighteen cells sit at 0.87 to 0.90 of every predicted step and afforded resolution, and 8-bit and 4-bit weights leave the threshold where bfloat16 puts it, so the tolerance is the registered minimum, 1.5, and the weight bar can hold only as an equality. ## G3c. A judge's symbol budget predicts its resolution before it is measured

Registration, 2026-09-18 to 2026-09-19. `experiments/G3c/`. Judges grade worksheets of twenty multiplications with a known number of wrong answers on scales of 1 to 5, 0 to 9 and 0 to 100, with six read-outs per scale (greedy score, expected score, and the mean of 1, 2, 4 or 8 exact samples from the judge's distribution, computed on 0 to 100 by a digit tree), plus pairwise grading in both orders. A calibration block predicts each read-out's accuracy at every gap, and the test block, from a seed drawn after the predictions are committed, grades it. Pilot on Atlas (Qwen 7B bfloat16, pilot seeds): every read-out within 0.088 of its prediction, largest z 2.88, the nominal rival 60 to 200 times worse, tau 0.95; all observed thresholds 1 to 21 percent above prediction, which the block-score check (chi-square p 0.57 to 0.64) and a null simulation that keeps the read-outs' shared test pairs (the pilot at the 59th to 83rd percentile) place as an ordinary draw. ### G9, a measurement finding that reaches back into G2

`articles/2026-09-19-the-hull-law-on-a-plane.md`. Both gates that have measured the hull law measured it on two coordinates, and neither chose to. A violation needs a point inside the hull of others, and by Radon's theorem a menu in d dimensions needs d + 2 points to be testable at all. G9's four actions in a five-dimensional consequence space are untestable there, measured at 0 testable menus in 4,000 trials, so the plane is the only place the law can be read.

Projection preserves hull membership and can create it, because convex combination survives any linear map. So violations can only increase under projection, never decrease. Measured in G9's exact configuration, rankings generated by genuine convex evaluators in five dimensions violate the law at 0.1506 when read on two coordinates, where their true rate is zero.

This changes what the G9 verdicts are worth without changing any of them. The primary plane's 0.0199 is an order of magnitude below the manufactured rate, so its pass is stronger than it looked. The refuting plane's 0.3000 is about twice the manufactured rate, so projection contributes and does not account for it, and the refutation survives at reduced strength.

It also bounds G2. That gate's 0.503 of testable respondents violating was read through pairs of issue scales, so the same inflation applies and its "about half" should be read as an upper bound on the true rate rather than an estimate of it. By how much is open and is not estimated.

## G9b. Was the G9 refutation projection?

G9 refuted the hull law on one plane, expected points added against first down probability, at 0.3000 against a null of 0.2211, and GET-12w records that the cause is open. The measurement was then found to inflate violations: projection preserves convex hull membership and can create it, so reading on two coordinates adds violations and never removes them, at 0.1506 on synthetic evaluators in G9's configuration against a true rate of zero.

The obvious remedy was to stop projecting, and the probe killed it. Radon's bound of d + 2 is necessary and nowhere near sufficient, because the consequence coordinates are dependent and the points lie near a low-dimensional curved set where nothing is inside anything's hull. Thirteen actions give 3 testable units in five dimensions and seven give none.

So the gate projects less rather than not at all. The primary claim is about the chain of subsets holding both of the refuting plane's coordinates. If the rate falls to its null as coordinates are added, the refutation was the plane. If it stays above, the refutation belongs to the decisions and GET-12w stands as a live counterexample to the hull law.

## G7a. The threshold tracks the time budget, in the same players across chess clocks
