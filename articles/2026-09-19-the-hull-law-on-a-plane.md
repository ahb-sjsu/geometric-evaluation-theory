# The hull law on a plane: why G2 and G9 had to project, and what projecting costs

An article of the Geometric Evaluation Theory repository, written 2026-09-19 after gate G9.
It concerns how the hull law has been measured in both gates that have measured it, not what
the law says. Everything below is labeled proved, measured, or open. The measurements are
`experiments/G9/g9_probe4.json`, the self-tests in `experiments/G9/g9_hull_law.py`, and a
projection check whose numbers appear in Section 3.

---

## 1. Both gates tested on two coordinates, and neither of them chose to

G2 tested the hull law on pairs of issue scales. G9 tested it on pairs of consequence
coordinates. In both cases the registration presents this as a way of keeping the test
non-vacuous, and in both cases that is true. It is worth saying why, because the reason is
sharper than "higher dimensions are sparse".

A violation requires a point to lie inside the convex hull of others. Points in general
position in `R^d` are affinely independent whenever there are at most `d + 1` of them, and an
affinely independent set has no point inside the hull of the rest. By Radon's theorem `d + 2`
points in `R^d` can always be split into two sets whose hulls meet, so `d + 2` is the smallest
menu that can be testable at all.

G9 has four actions and a five-dimensional consequence space. Four is not seven, so the law is
not merely hard to violate in that space. It is **untestable** there. Measured on 4,000 random
menus whose rankings came from genuine convex evaluators in five dimensions, the number of
testable menus in the full space was **0**.

So the plane is not a convenience in G9. It is the only place the law can be read at all with
four actions. G2, with five candidates on two scales, sits just above the Radon bound and is
testable for the same arithmetic reason.

## 2. Projection can only add violations

**Proved.** Let `P` be linear. If `y_a` lies in the convex hull of a set, so `y_a = sum_s
lambda_s y_s` with `lambda >= 0` summing to one, then `P y_a = sum_s lambda_s P y_s`, which
lies in the hull of the projected set. Hull membership is therefore preserved by projection.
The converse fails: a point outside a hull can land inside it after projection.

A violation is made of hull membership. So for a fixed ranking, the set of violations in a
projection contains the set of violations in the full space. **Projecting can create violations
and can never destroy them.**

An explicit witness, four points in `R^3` in convex position, none inside the hull of the other
three. Dropping the third coordinate puts the fourth inside the triangle of the first three.

    (0,0,0)  (1,0,0)  (0,1,0)  (0.3,0.3,1.0)

## 3. What it costs, measured in G9's own configuration

Four actions, a five-dimensional consequence space, a ranking generated by a genuine convex
quadratic evaluator in that space, and the hull law then read on two of the five coordinates,
which is exactly what the gate does. 4,000 trials.

| where the law is read | testable menus | violating | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| the evaluator's own five dimensions | 0 of 4,000 | 0 | undefined, never testable |
| two of the five coordinates | 1,454 of 4,000 | 219 | **0.1506** |

Every violation in the second row is manufactured. The rankings came from convex evaluators,
which cannot violate the law in the space they live in.

The figure is a reference point and not a baseline to subtract. It is computed on Gaussian
point configurations and random evaluators, and football's menus are neither. It gives an order
of magnitude, which is what the argument needs.

## 4. How this changes the reading of G9

The gate's own shuffle null already accounts for the geometry of each menu, since it permutes
rankings within the configuration, so nothing here changes a verdict. What changes is how much
the verdicts are worth.

| plane | observed | shuffle null | manufactured rate from Section 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected points, turnover probability (primary) | 0.0199 | 0.2286 | about 0.15 |
| expected points, first down probability (refutes) | 0.3000 | 0.2211 | about 0.15 |

**The primary pass is stronger than it looked.** Football's coaches violate the law on that
plane at 0.0199, which is an order of magnitude below the rate that random convex evaluators
appear to violate at when read the same way. Whatever is generating those play calls is not
merely convex-compatible, it is cleaner on this plane than a generic convex evaluator would
look through the same lens.

**The refutation survives, at reduced strength.** Projection alone manufactures about 0.15 and
the failing plane shows 0.3000, roughly twice that. So projection contributes to it and does
not account for it. Saying "a fail on a plane is weak evidence" was right in direction and too
generous in size, and this is the correction.

## 5. How this reaches back into G2

G2's headline is that 0.503 of 489 testable respondents violate against a shuffle null of
0.664, and its record states plainly that the law holds there as a population tendency and
fails as a deterministic statement for about half the respondents.

That was measured on two-dimensional pairs drawn from four issue scales. A respondent who
evaluates candidates on all four scales, or on those plus party and personality, is being read
through a projection of their own evaluation space, and Section 2 says that can only inflate
the violation count.

**Open.** By how much is not known and is not estimated here. It would take testing the same
respondents on all four scales at once, where five candidates in four dimensions sits just
above the Radon bound of six and is therefore thin but not empty. Until then, G2's "about half"
should be read as an upper bound on the true violation rate rather than an estimate of it.

## 6. What would settle it, and why the obvious answer does not

**Superseded the same day. Read Section 7.**

The obvious answer is more actions, so the law can be read in the space the evaluator actually
uses. For a five-dimensional consequence space the menu must hold at least seven actions to be
testable at all, and comfortably more to be testable often. The nflverse data supports this
directly: run location crossed with run gap, and pass length crossed with pass location, give
nine to thirteen play types in place of the four this gate used.

## 7. Correction, 2026-09-19: Radon's bound is necessary and nowhere near sufficient

Section 6 was written before it was measured, and it is wrong. `experiments/G9b/g9b_probe.py`
built those larger action spaces and counted testable units in the full five-dimensional space.

| action space | actions | cells carrying every action | testable units in full 5-D |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 play types | 13 | 126 | 3 |
| 9 play types | 9 | 151 | 12 |
| 7 play types | 7 | 175 | 0 |

Seven actions clears the bound of `d + 2` and produces nothing at all. Thirteen produces three,
against G2's 489 testable respondents and G9's 402 testable units.

The bound counts points and ignores where they sit. The five consequence coordinates are
strongly dependent, so real consequence points lie close to a low-dimensional curved set inside
the five-dimensional space, and a point on a surface is almost never strictly inside the hull of
other points on that surface. Adding actions adds points to the same surface.

This makes Section 2's conclusion worse rather than better. Projection is not an avoidable
artifact of how G2 and G9 happened to be designed. It is forced by the geometry of real
consequence spaces, and the hull law may be practically untestable in any consequence space
whose coordinates are as dependent as these are.

**Open, and reframed.** The question is no longer whether projection can be avoided. It is what
the violation rate does as less of it is applied, which is measurable on this data by walking
the dimension ladder from two coordinates to five. That measurement is in progress and is not
reported here.
